How to read this

One town is one run. Every town here was made by the same rules from a different seed, so the differences between towns under one setting are luck, and the spread shows how much luck there is.

Failed means what it means everywhere in this model: the first week that the set share of all people (90% as written) are unhappy. A town stops there.

The middle is the median town. The middle half runs from the first quartile to the third, in a straight line between towns. With few towns these are rough.

Still standing. A figure for a year covers only the towns that had not failed by the end of it. A town that fails in the last week of a year is not in that year's figure. Where few are left the figure rests on few towns, and each chart says how many.

The year of failure is given two ways. The middle of the towns that failed says nothing of the towns that never did. "Half of all the towns had failed by" counts them, and is the one to compare settings by.

A class with nobody in it has no figure that year, so it rests on fewer towns still.

The need whose price had risen most is, of the four base needs, the one whose price was highest against its starting price in the town's last week. Needs within a tenth of a percent of the highest are counted as level with it.

To see one town in full. Every person and firm is recorded once a quarter in a batch town; every event and every weekly measure is kept. Its dashboard and town view can be built from its folder: python -m engine dashboard FOLDER and python -m engine town FOLDER. To make the same town again with every person recorded every week, from its world's folder: python run.py --config FOLDER/config.json --set snapshot_every=1. It has the same record fingerprint.
