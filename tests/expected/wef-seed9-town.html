    How to read this

    Recorded, every week. Where each person lives and works, which food firm and which entertainment firm they pay, their class, their wellbeing, their cash and their pay, and whether they pay for a ride. Each firm's spot, staff, customers and price. The numbers in the panel.

    Recorded, once a quarter. What stands on each spot. Between those records a firm is drawn from the week it has its spot, and homes from the week someone lives there.

    Drawn, not recorded. The journeys. The model works in whole weeks and has no days, so each week is drawn as one round: home, work, the food shop, the entertainment firm, home. Journeys are drawn slower than they would be so that they can be seen. A person who pays for a ride moves 4 times as fast as a walker, as in the model. A round too long to fit in the week is hurried so that it does. Where a dot sits inside a block is also drawn: it is that person's own place, the same every week.

    No home. The model gives a person with no home no place on the map. They are drawn in the strip under the map, and their round starts and ends there.

    One person. The lines beside a person have one point a quarter. The number beside each line is its latest point, and the page says which week that point is. What happened to them is from the record. Something that happened several times in one quarter is told once, with how many times.

    The card. Point at a person to see their card. Under their class it gives their cash and their pay. Cash is what they hold at the end of the week shown, after pay and bills. Pay is what their job pays a week. Dividends are not pay and are not on the card. Both numbers are for the week shown, so the cash can differ from the line beside a person, which has one point a quarter.

    Class is by role. Owner class: founders, anyone who controls a firm, chief executives, executives, and people living on dividends alone. Working class: staff drawing a wage, with or without shares. Out of work: everyone else. Unhappy means wellbeing below 0.5. The town fails when 90% of everyone is unhappy.

    Keys. Space plays and pauses. The left and right arrows step back and forward.
